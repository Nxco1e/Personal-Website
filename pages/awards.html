Leadership & Community

Leadership rooted in mentorship, access, and helping others move forward

Mentorship

Girls Who Code — AI+ Peer Mentor

My Role: I mentor high school students as they build their confidence and skills in technology, including programming, artificial intelligence, Python, Java, problem-solving, and responsible technology.

Rather than simply giving students answers, I try to help them understand how to approach problems themselves. One of my favorite parts of mentoring is seeing someone go from thinking they can't solve something to realizing they can.

How It Has Helped Me Grow: Girls Who Code has made me a stronger technical communicator. Knowing how to solve a problem and knowing how to explain that problem to someone else are completely different skills.

It has also made giving back a much bigger part of how I think about my career. I know what it feels like to enter technology without a roadmap, so I want younger students to feel like there is a place for them in the field.

Event Leadership

Brooklyn College Computer Science Club — Event Coordinator

My Role: As an Event Coordinator, I help organize workshops, networking opportunities, guest speakers, and events that expose students to technology and professional opportunities beyond the classroom.

The role involves coordinating with students, faculty members, and professionals while managing communication, promotion, logistics, and participation.

How It Has Helped Me Grow: This position has strengthened my organization, communication, networking, and event-planning abilities.

More importantly, it has taught me that leadership doesn't always mean being the loudest person in the room. Sometimes leadership means creating the room in the first place — bringing people together and giving them an opportunity to learn from one another.

Student Support

CUNY Career Connect / Brooklyn College — Career Ambassador

My Role: As a Career Ambassador, I help students find internships, professional development programs, career resources, and counselors who can support their goals.

This role is personal to me because I entered college without a professional roadmap myself. As a first-generation student at a non-target school, I had to learn where opportunities were, how recruiting worked, how to network, and what resources were available.

Once I started figuring those things out, I wanted to make the process easier for someone else.

How It Has Helped Me Grow: Being a Career Ambassador has strengthened my communication and leadership abilities while teaching me how much access to information matters.

I've learned that someone can be incredibly capable and ambitious but simply not know an opportunity exists. That's why sharing resources has become such an important part of how I approach leadership.

Governance

Brooklyn College Undergraduate Student Government — Club Funding Council Member

My Role: As a Club Funding Council Member, I review student organization funding requests and budgets, evaluate whether proposals follow university policies, consider financial and operational risks, and contribute to responsible funding decisions.

The position combines several areas I'm interested in: finance, governance, risk, decision-making, and community involvement.

How It Has Helped Me Grow: This role has taught me to make decisions based on evidence rather than simply personal preference.

I've gained experience thinking about accountability, governance, financial responsibility, policy, and risk. Those skills also connect directly with my growing interest in cybersecurity risk and governance.

Mentorship

Mentors in Tech — Fellow

My Role: As a Mentors in Tech Fellow, I worked with industry mentors while developing my professional communication, interviewing, networking, technical problem-solving, and understanding of careers in technology.

As someone who didn't enter college with an established professional network in tech, having professionals I could ask questions and learn from was extremely valuable.

How It Has Helped Me Grow: Mentors in Tech taught me that I don't need to figure everything out alone.

I've become much more comfortable asking questions, seeking feedback, reaching out to professionals, and admitting when I don't know something. Experiencing good mentorship also made me want to provide that same support to other students.

Professional Growth

McKinsey Forward Program — Participant

My Role: Through the McKinsey Forward Program, I developed skills around structured problem-solving, communication, adaptability, relationship building, and digital and AI concepts.

The experience exposed me to new ways of breaking down ambiguous problems and communicating solutions to different audiences.

How It Has Helped Me Grow: The program helped me understand that being technically correct isn't always enough. You also need to understand the problem, organize your thinking, communicate your reasoning, and make your solution understandable to the people affected by it.

That has become particularly relevant to my interest in cyber risk, where technical risks often need to be communicated to business leaders.

Community

Women in CyberSecurity (WiCyS) — Member

My Role: Through WiCyS, I became part of a broader community of women pursuing careers across cybersecurity.

Being exposed to professionals and students across different areas of security has helped me understand how many directions a cybersecurity career can take.

How It Has Helped Me Grow: Being part of WiCyS has helped me become more comfortable with the fact that I'm still learning.

Cybersecurity is enormous, and it can sometimes feel like everyone else knows more than you. Being around other women at different stages of their careers has reminded me that you don't need to know everything before you belong in the field.

Collaboration

CodeDay — Open-Source Contributor

Leadership/Community Component: Although CodeDay was primarily a technical experience, working on an open-source project required collaboration, communication, receiving feedback, asking for help, and contributing to something larger than my individual work.

I worked on Serverless DNS under the mentorship of a Senior Software Engineer at Bloomberg and collaborated through GitHub workflows, code reviews, testing, and pull requests.

How It Has Helped Me Grow: CodeDay taught me to become comfortable being uncomfortable technically. I learned that I could enter an unfamiliar codebase, ask questions, make mistakes, accept feedback, and eventually make a meaningful contribution.

That confidence has carried into almost every technical experience I've pursued afterward.

The Leadership Story Behind All of It

Opportunity is meaningful when it is shared

The common thread across my leadership experiences is that I didn't start out feeling like a leader.

As a first-generation student at a non-target school, I spent a lot of my early college experience trying to figure things out myself. I searched for internships, joined programs, reached out to professionals, found mentors, asked questions, and slowly built the network and knowledge I didn't start with.

As I learned more, my mindset changed from "How do I find these opportunities?" to "How can I make sure someone else knows these opportunities exist too?"

That's why many of my leadership roles revolve around mentorship, access, community, and professional development. Whether I'm helping a high school student understand code, connecting a Brooklyn College student with an internship, organizing an event for the Computer Science Club, or helping make responsible funding decisions through Student Government, I want my leadership to make something easier for the person coming after me.

The opportunities I've received have helped me move forward. My goal as a leader is to make sure I bring other people forward with me.

Work Experience

Each experience has shaped how I think about technology, cybersecurity, risk, and leadership

My experiences have helped me build a foundation in technology, cybersecurity, risk, and leadership. Each one challenged me to learn quickly, navigate unfamiliar environments, and apply technical knowledge to real-world problems.

From hospital operations and DNS infrastructure to cyber risk and student mentorship, these roles have shaped the direction I want to pursue in the future.

Ferrosource

Cyber Risk Analyst Intern

June 2026 – August 2026

New York, NY

At Ferrosource, I worked at the intersection of cybersecurity, data integrity, and operational risk. I reviewed internal reporting workflows, identified discrepancies and duplicate records, and helped strengthen controls around sensitive business data.

Using JavaScript, Node.js, GitHub, Claude Code, Excel, and ENITEO ERP systems, I built and tested automated internal workflows designed to improve accuracy and reduce risk. This experience pushed me to think beyond whether code worked — I also had to consider whether the data was trustworthy, protected, and aligned with secure business practices.

What I learned: Ferrosource expanded the way I think about cybersecurity. A large part of security is not just responding to attacks, but also preventing operational and data risks before they become business problems. It also reinforced how closely cybersecurity connects to finance, governance, and reliable technology systems.

CodeDay

Software Engineering Intern — Serverless DNS

June 2026 – August 2026

Remote

Through CodeDay, I contributed to Serverless DNS, an open-source project focused on DNS, privacy, and secure internet infrastructure. I worked with JavaScript, Node.js, Git, GitHub, VS Code, and Replit to validate DNS-over-HTTPS functionality, test resolver behavior, and improve secure DNS-related features.

This experience also exposed me to collaborative software development in a real-world setting, including branching, pull requests, debugging, and code review feedback.

What I learned: CodeDay pushed me further into the technical side of cybersecurity. Working with DNS made me more curious about the protocols, networks, and infrastructure that power the internet behind the scenes. It also strengthened my ability to learn in unfamiliar environments and keep iterating when something does not work on the first try.

NYC Health + Hospitals / Woodhull

Engineering Operations Intern

March 2026 – May 2026

Brooklyn, NY

At NYC Health + Hospitals/Woodhull, I supported the Engineering Operations team with technical documentation, preventive maintenance records, scheduling, and cross-team coordination. I also helped organize documentation in preparation for Joint Commission readiness.

Working in a hospital setting gave me a firsthand understanding of how much an organization depends on procedures, compliance, and accurate records to operate safely and effectively.

What I learned: Woodhull showed me that security is not only technical. It also depends on documentation, process discipline, accountability, and strong operational controls. This experience strengthened my interest in cyber risk and the organizational side of security.

CUNY Career Connect / Brooklyn College

Career Ambassador

September 2025 – Present

Brooklyn, NY

As a Career Ambassador, I help students connect with internships, career resources, and professional development opportunities. I also guide them through the process of identifying the right next step for their goals.

Because I’ve had to navigate many of these same challenges myself as a first-generation student, this role is especially meaningful to me. I want to make the process more accessible for other students who may not yet know where to begin.

What I learned: This experience reinforced how much access to information, mentorship, and opportunity can shape someone’s trajectory. It also strengthened my communication and leadership skills while reminding me that helping others succeed is part of the work I want to do.

Girls Who Code

AI+ Peer Mentor

Mentorship role

As a Girls Who Code AI+ Peer Mentor, I worked with high school students on Python, Java, AI, and problem-solving, helping them build confidence in technical concepts that often feel intimidating at first.

My goal was not just to provide answers, but to help students feel capable of thinking through problems on their own and continuing when they were stuck.

What I learned: Mentoring strengthened my ability to explain technical ideas clearly and adapt my communication based on the person I am helping. It also reinforced my belief that making technology feel more approachable is an essential part of opening doors for others.
